An approved task can earn you a stake in the organization behind it. That stake can count in future decisions and revenue distributions, so your contribution has a place beyond the finished work.

A task shows its contribution reward and any funded payment before you take it on. You earn the configured rewards when a reviewer approves completion.

## See the agreement before you begin

The Decentral Park task below offers 50 shares for propagating plant cuttings and installing them in a shared space. The description, submitted result, and reward are visible together. In Poa, these shares are participation units that record a stake in that organization.

![Decentral Park plant propagation task showing its description, submission, and 50-share reward](/images/product/task-detail.webp "Decentral Park: the displayed 50 shares are a contribution reward, separate from a cash payment.")

The group chooses which work to reward and how much it earns. That makes room for contributions people might otherwise overlook, from hosting an event to patiently answering support questions.

## Payment now, a stake in what comes next

| A way your work can count | What makes it happen |
| --- | --- |
| Payment for a task | The task includes a funded payment, released when the work is approved and completed. |
| Ownership in the organization | Completing an approved task issues its assigned ownership reward. |
| Weight in a decision | The organization's voting rules use contribution balances to give eligible members a say. |
| A share of distributed revenue | The organization creates a funded distribution with recorded allocations that eligible members can claim. |

For example, suppose you hold 10% of the ownership at the snapshot used for a proportional distribution. In a distribution of 1,000 USDC based on that snapshot, your allocation would be 100 USDC.

The organization chooses when to distribute available funds. Each distribution records its own split; later contributions do not change an earlier snapshot. [The treasury guide](/docs/treasury-management) explains the process.

## Choose how work shapes decisions

A project can weight votes by contribution, give eligible members equal votes, or combine approaches. The reward balance is one input the group can choose to use; it does not dictate how every vote must work.

Think about the decision you want to make. Choosing a club's next topic may call for equal member voices. Deciding a software project's next investment may benefit from the experience of its active contributors. [Explore voting options](/docs/hybridVoting).

## How your share can change

Later work can earn new units, changing each person's percentage of the total. Suppose you hold 100 of the organization's 1,000 units: 10%. If other contributors earn another 1,000 units while your balance stays at 100, you now hold 5% of 2,000. None of your units moved to someone else. A later proportional distribution would use its own recorded allocation; the earlier distribution remains unchanged.

A balance and a role are separate. Units do not automatically make you a maintainer, give you review authority, or qualify you for every vote. Check the eligible roles and weighting in each voting class. Equal-member and blended voting can give newcomers a voice alongside accumulated contribution. [Roles and permissions](/docs/roles-and-permissions) explains how responsibilities are assigned.

Keep the reward policy understandable: for example, recognize documentation and review alongside code, name who approves each result, and explain why similar tasks earn different amounts.

## What your stake means

Ownership belongs to a specific Poa organization and is recorded in participation tokens. These units cannot be bought, sold, transferred, or given away. The group's permissions determine who can issue them and approve rewards.

The rights they carry come from the organization's software and rules. Legal shares, employment, copyright, and ownership of other assets need their own agreements where relevant.

When choosing a task, look at both its reward and the rules that give that reward meaning. [The task guide](/docs/task-manager) explains how work is assigned, reviewed, and completed.
