# Create an organization

Use Poa when people need a clear way to join, take responsibility for work, and decide how shared resources are used. It is especially useful when contributions should count beyond the task itself: in future decisions or an approved distribution of funds.

A club can use membership and polls without collecting money. A working team can add reviewed tasks, contribution rewards, and funded payments. Start with the parts your group will use now.

## Choose a setup around real work

Picture the next thing you want to finish: a repair day, a client project, or a software release. Write down who can help, who will review the results, and which decisions need the group. Those answers make the template and settings easier to choose.

For example, a repair group might welcome members openly, reserve review powers for experienced volunteers, and give eligible members equal votes on equipment purchases. A software team might separate contributors from maintainers and give recorded work more weight in roadmap decisions.

## Decide how people join and take responsibility

Open membership suits a group that wants people to arrive and get involved. Invitations suit a known team. Vouches let existing members help judge whether someone is ready for a role. Use the entry rules available in your chosen setup.

Keep joining separate from authority. Someone can be welcome to contribute without being able to approve their own work, change budgets, or manage membership. Assign the permissions each responsibility needs, and check voting eligibility separately.

## Choose what gives a vote weight

| Choose | When it fits | What to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Equal member weight | Members share responsibility for a club, workplace, or common space. | Every eligible member counts equally, including someone new. Be clear about who qualifies. |
| Contribution weight | Recorded work should influence the project's priorities. | Agree on rewards and review before relying on balances; established contributors can hold more weight. |
| A blend | Both membership and accumulated work should count. | Choose how much of the result each receives, then try the split against a real decision your group faces. |

These choices all work with [binding proposals](/docs/hybridVoting). A separate poll can gather preferences, but cannot move funds or change rules.

## Agree on rewards and useful extras

Set a [task's](/docs/task-manager) contribution reward before someone begins. Approved completion awards the configured participation units. Add a bounty when you want to offer a payment and can fund it. Later [distributions](/docs/treasury-management) are separate funded decisions with recorded allocations that recipients claim.

Enable learning modules if members need a repeatable introduction to your tools or processes. Enable elections if the group will elect people to roles. A template gives you initial choices; keep the ones that serve the work.

## Open setup and launch

You can [explore setup](/create) before signing in; you need an account to launch. Bring a name, a short description, and the initial team. The flow moves through **Template, Identity, Team, Governance, Settings, and Launch**. The [step-by-step guide](/docs/deployment-wizard) explains each control.

For a new account, choose **Create Account**, enter a username, and follow your device's fingerprint, face, or PIN prompt. Returning members can use **Sign In**; **Connect Wallet** is also available for an existing wallet.

The launch review shows the organization's network; new organizations currently default to Gnosis. Check **Gas Sponsorship** in Settings if the group will cover members' transaction fees, and review its initial funding before launch. The [sponsorship guide](/docs/gas-sponsor) explains how that support works.

Review the final settings, then launch. Prepare one useful task and its reviewer before sharing the organization link. Explain how to join and what someone can do first; [your first week on Poa](/docs/first-week) gives a worked example.
