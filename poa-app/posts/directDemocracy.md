# Equal member voting and polls

Use a poll to choose a meeting time, compare ideas, or ask what the group wants next. Each eligible member's vote counts equally, regardless of contribution balance. To spend funds or change a rule, use a binding proposal instead.

## Use a poll to understand the group

The direct-democracy polling system records preferences. It does not transfer money, assign roles, or change organizational rules.

Who can create and vote in a poll depends on the organization's permissions and any role restrictions on that poll. “Equal” describes the weight among eligible voters; it does not mean every account on Poa can participate.

## Choose the next outing

A hiking club wants to choose its next outing. A member opens a poll with three routes, describing the distance, travel time, and accessibility of each.

After members vote and the result is confirmed, the organizers use the chosen route to plan the trip.

If the club also needs to book transport, it prepares a separate spending proposal with an amount, recipient, and explanation.

## Equal weight can also govern actions

An organization can give an equal-member class 100% of a binding vote's weight. That configuration can make decisions about spending and rules while retaining equal weight among the members included in the class.

These proposals use the [hybrid voting system](/docs/hybridVoting), even when contribution weight is zero. The system's name describes what it can support, rather than requiring every organization to use a blend.

## Make participation meaningful

Use clear options, give members enough context, and allow time to respond. Explain who will act on a poll's result and whether a binding proposal will follow. Members should know what their participation can change.

Review the configured proposal threshold and minimum voter count before opening a vote. A result with little participation may offer less guidance, even if it meets the formal rules.

Equal voting also relies on a trusted membership process. Agree on who belongs and how new members join, particularly before decisions with lasting effects. [Vouching](/docs/vouching-and-trust) and other membership rules can help make that process explicit.
