# Vouching and trust

If a role requires vouches, check how many you need and who can give them. A vouch records an eligible member's support for you joining that role. After meeting the requirements, you may still need to claim or accept it.

## Start with a shared meaning

Explain what a vouch means in your organization. “I have worked with this person and trust them to review documentation” is more useful than an undefined endorsement.

Define who can vouch, how many vouches are required, and what the role allows its holder to do. Use the actual settings shown for the role. Configuration and available options differ between access-system versions.

A vouch helps a group use personal judgment. It is not a guarantee of someone's future behavior or a complete defense against duplicate accounts and coordinated abuse.

## From candidate to member

1. A candidate chooses the role and follows any application steps shown.
2. The candidate shares enough context for eligible members to make a considered decision.
3. Members record their vouches through the available profile or application flow.
4. Once the requirements are met, the candidate follows the claim or acceptance action.
5. The candidate checks that the role is active and understands its responsibilities.

## Welcome a new reviewer

A neighborhood repair group requires two eligible reviewers to vouch for a new Reviewer. Maya has helped at several repair sessions and wants to take responsibility for reviewing completed tasks.

Dani and Sam read her application and vouch based on their experience working together. When Maya's role becomes available, she accepts it. The group now has another person who can review work under its configured permissions.

At the next session, Maya can take on reviews that used to wait for Dani or Sam.

## Trust can change

Check the rules before treating a role as permanent. In organizations using the newer membership authority, active membership depends on both acceptance and continuing eligibility. Revoked vouches or changed requirements can affect eligibility when no other qualifying rule applies. A reconciliation action can then update a membership whose requirements are no longer met.

Other organizations use an earlier role system with different vouch and removal behavior. Follow the current role status and available actions rather than assuming a vouch always lasts or always disappears when a voucher leaves.

Some explicitly protected governance grants can only be changed by a vote. Other roles can be managed by authorized members. [Roles and permissions](/docs/roles-and-permissions) explains that distinction.

## Keep the door understandable

Publish the criteria, help newcomers find eligible vouchers, and give applicants a clear next step. Offer a way to meet members through a small project or shared session, so people arriving without personal connections can build that trust too. Review whether the process is sharing responsibility or simply making it hard to join.

Use vouching where that introduction helps the group judge readiness. Open membership may be enough for a discussion role; a sensitive responsibility may need an invitation or governance decision.
