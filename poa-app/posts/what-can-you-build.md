Poa becomes useful when a group wants its work, decisions, and resources to belong to an organization people can help shape. These six examples show different ways to arrange that relationship. They are possibilities you can adapt, not accounts of existing customers.

## A campus club ready for its next leaders

A campus blockchain club runs discussions, builds small tools, and pools money for workshops. Each spring, experienced members graduate. The next team needs more than a calendar: it needs to know which commitments remain, who can welcome members, and how to spend the club's funds.

The club could give eligible members equal weight in binding decisions, with separate coordinator and reviewer responsibilities. A spending proposal can authorize a workshop payment; an authorized membership change can bring another organizer into responsibility. The club keeps a record of both as its people change.

Contribution rewards are optional here. A discussion club may value equal membership without tying its decisions to how many tasks someone completed. [Explore community groups](/docs/community-groups).

## A studio where each delivery builds something lasting

Three freelancers deliver websites together. A fourth joins to write content and manage client handover. They want that work to count in the studio, as well as earn a payment for the project.

Tasks can offer funded bounties and participation rewards separately. The team could use a blend of equal member votes and contribution weight, so newcomers have a voice while accumulated work also matters. When there are funds to share, members can approve a distribution based on recorded participation balances.

The content writer can gain influence over which projects the studio pursues and an allocation in a funded distribution. These are rights within the Poa organization; legal ownership and employment need separate agreements. [See the studio example](/docs/worker-cooperatives).

## An open-source library with a funded roadmap

An accessibility library has users, a growing issue list, and money available for maintenance. Contributors want to improve keyboard navigation; others want a new component. Someone has to choose where the budget goes.

The collective could reward accepted code, documentation, and review work with participation units, then use contribution-weighted voting among eligible members to approve funded work. A separate reviewer role checks completed tasks. The people maintaining the library gain a defined way to influence its investment.

Issues and pull requests stay in the repository. Poa records the reward and spending decisions around them; a merge does not automatically award participation units. [Organize an open-source collective](/docs/open-source-collectives).

## A repair group that can share responsibility

A neighborhood group holds monthly repair afternoons. Its volunteers need spare parts, a room, and enough experienced people to review completed work. Public tasks can show what needs preparing; shared funds can pay agreed expenses.

Open membership could make it easy to help, while vouches from experienced members provide a path into a reviewer role. Equal-member binding votes could govern spending. This lets another volunteer take on real responsibility through rules the group understands.

Keep residents' contact details and personal circumstances in private channels. If the group also provides mutual aid, allocate help according to need separately from contribution rewards. [Prepare a community event](/docs/first-week).

## A makerspace whose maintenance earns recognition

A makerspace wants a new machine, but keeping the current equipment usable already takes work. Members who repair tools, write guides, or organize sessions can earn participation units for approved tasks.

The group could combine equal member weight with contribution weight when choosing an equipment budget. Every eligible member has a voice, and work that sustains the space can also count. A learning module can introduce workshop procedures; practical training and physical access remain the group's responsibility.

The useful connection is between caring for the space and helping direct its future. [Explore contribution and ownership](/docs/contribution-and-ownership).

## A research group that recognizes the work behind a finding

An independent research group is comparing low-cost air-quality sensors. Some members design the experiment; others reproduce measurements, clean data, or document limitations.

Poa tasks can recognize each accepted contribution. The group might keep research priorities on equal-member votes while using participation balances for a separately approved distribution if it has funds to share. Scientific review stays in the group's research process; a contribution balance does not establish whether a finding is correct.

Public work records could also support a custom progress report, with sensitive data kept elsewhere. That report would need to be built, but it could read the organization's existing records. [Work with public organization data](/docs/TheGraph).

## Choose the arrangement that serves the work

Equal membership, contribution weight, funded tasks, and distributions serve different purposes. Use the parts your group needs and explain how they fit together. The [setup guide](/docs/deployment-wizard) shows where to make those choices.
