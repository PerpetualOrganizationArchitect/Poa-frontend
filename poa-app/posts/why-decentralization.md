# Decentralization and the freedom to build

Your group may want its own member site, a release dashboard, or an interface built around the way you work. You can develop those tools without recreating the underlying Poa organization.

Poa separates the organization's shared records and contract rules from the website used to reach them. You can start with the hosted app, then build another way in as your needs develop.

## Choose your front door

A campus club might want a familiar university community address. A studio might put its member space beside its portfolio. A software collective might maintain a version of the interface that fits its own workflow.

Poa supports configured custom domains and an independently hosted open-source frontend. Changing the entry point does not require creating a new organization. Your existing members, decisions, and funds remain connected to the same underlying records.

[The hosting guide](/docs/white-label-hosting) explains the setup and maintenance involved. A custom domain gives you a different address; a maintained independent frontend gives you more control over the interface itself.

## Put the record to work

An open-source maintainer could build a release dashboard from completed tasks. A studio could give members a view of decisions across its projects. A new tool could read open work without becoming the place that owns the record.

Poa's [public data layer](/docs/TheGraph) makes independent views possible. A developer can query indexed records, retrieve referenced content, and check results against the underlying network. Integrations still need to be built and maintained, but they can use a common source of organization activity.

The same records help a member check who approved a task or trace a spending decision to its payment.

## Understand what makes this possible

The website connects to public contracts that record and enforce organization actions. Longer descriptions and other content use separate storage, including IPFS. Compatible tools can work with those same contracts and content under the organization's permissions.

This separation supports censorship resistance by reducing dependence on a single interface or service. If one front door becomes unavailable, a maintained alternative can still reach the underlying organization. That alternative still depends on working accounts, services, and network access.

## Keep your alternatives usable

Independence takes maintenance. Keep access to accounts, working network connections, available content, and someone who understands the configuration. Hosting providers and gateways can refuse requests, networks can have outages, and payment assets may have issuer controls. Decentralization does not make every action impossible to block.

Public records also deserve thoughtful inputs. Keep credentials, private addresses, and sensitive personal details out of tasks and proposals. A neighborhood group can record a delivery budget without publishing recipients' circumstances.

The hosted app is one way to use this infrastructure. [Create an organization](/docs/create) there, and use the hosting and data guides when you need another interface.
