# Poa for worker cooperatives

You already know people whose work makes yours better. Together, you could take on a project none of you would attempt alone—and build a studio around the work you want to do.

Poa gives the studio a shared record of its work, rewards, decisions, and funds. A client project can pay for today's effort while accepted contributions earn a stake in the business you are developing together.

## Deliver a client project

Imagine a designer, developer, and strategist who regularly deliver websites together. For a larger project, a writer joins to develop the content and handle the client guide. The team wants a clear agreement about both project pay and the writer's place in the studio.

They create [tasks](/docs/task-manager) for discovery, design, development, content, testing, and handover. Each says what a finished result looks like and who reviews it. The content task can include a [funded bounty](/docs/treasury-management#deposit-and-plan) for delivery and participation units that record a stake in the organization. Approval and completion award the configured rewards.

Include the work around delivery too: writing the proposal, talking to the client, and keeping the books. That is part of building the studio. Keep confidential deliverables in your team's private tools; the [task guide](/docs/task-manager#turn-a-project-into-work-people-can-take) explains what goes on the public record.

By handover, the client has a finished website. The writer has earned the agreed rewards and can see how their contribution counts under the studio's voting and distribution rules. Their relationship with the studio can continue beyond this invoice.

## Decide how the studio operates

The studio may want to buy equipment, fund its own product, or give another member authority to approve work. Members can bring a proposal with a clear purpose and discuss it before voting.

For this studio, a blend could give equal member voting half the result and contribution weight the other half. The writer gets an equal member voice once eligible, while the team's accumulated work also counts. The group chooses the eligible roles and passing requirements. An entirely equal-member model is available too.

A [binding proposal](/docs/hybridVoting) can authorize supported spending or a permission change, with an execution result members can check. The studio can therefore put its agreed way of making decisions into practice.

## Separate project payments from shared revenue

A funded task bounty pays for a particular outcome. A treasury distribution lets eligible recipients claim an approved allocation of shared funds. Your cooperative can use distributions to share revenue after deciding what to keep for expenses and future work.

Suppose the studio chooses to distribute 2,000 USDC after reserving its expenses. If the writer holds 10% of the participation balance used for that proportional allocation, their claim would be 200 USDC. Members approve that specific funded distribution; later work does not change its snapshot.

This payment is separate from the task bounty and is not recurring or guaranteed. The [treasury guide](/docs/treasury-management) covers funding, approval, and claims.

Poa records participation rights within the software. Legal ownership and employment arrangements need their own agreements. [Contribution and ownership](/docs/contribution-and-ownership) explains how the pieces fit.

## Bring your team in

Before inviting the team, outline the delivery and agree on rewards, reviewers, and how much funding the work needs. [Create the organization](/docs/create) around that agreement. After handover, use the completed work and spending record to review how the project went before taking on another.
